Car Accident Lawyer · Law Reference

Resources

A directory of the legal, insurance, health, and road-safety sources behind the topic.

Public agencies, legal references, and research organizations support different parts of a car accident claim. Their roles are distinct: a crash dataset does not determine fault, and a general legal definition does not calculate a state filing deadline.

Resource directory

Crash and injury statistics

NHTSA publishes road-fatality and police-reported-crash data. CDC adds health and injury context; BLS reports work-related fatal injuries.

Read the statistical evidence →

Insurance and coverage

NAIC explains coverage and consumer claim processes. IRC and Triple-I provide distinct insurance-market and paid-claim datasets.

Compare coverage types →

Legal concepts and liability

Cornell's Legal Information Institute explains negligence, comparative fault, subrogation and related doctrines. State law determines application.

Review the legal concepts →

Lawyer licensing and fees

ABA directories identify state licensing authorities. Its model rules explain fee and communication concepts; jurisdictions adopt their own rules.

Evaluate legal representation →

Medical care and payment issues

CDC explains concussion symptoms and danger signs. CMS and IRS materials address different payment and tax questions.

Read about medical documentation →

Safety research

AAA Foundation research models drowsy-driving involvement. IIHS studies vehicle safety technology. Preserve each study's dates and limits.

Read the research context →

State-based provider resources

The Advocates in Colorado and The Advocates in Idaho publish information about their state-based personal injury services. Verify the lawyer, service area and engagement terms directly. These links are not comparative rankings.

Using the sources

The full reference list below matches the main article. URLs are intentionally plain text; copy a URL into a browser to inspect its source. Check observation dates, publication status and population before reusing a statistic. For an individual legal question, identify the governing state authority and the relevant facts.
